Electrical Work in Glencoe Homes

Glencoe's housing stock runs to two extremes, and we work on both. On one end are the grand homes from the 1910s and 1920s — Tudors, colonials, and the Frank Lloyd Wright-designed houses of Ravine Bluffs — set on deep, wooded lots near the lake and the ravines. Many still carry portions of their original wiring under layers of remodels done across a century. Cloth-insulated conductors, junction boxes buried behind plaster, and services sized for a household that owned one radio are things we find here regularly.

On the other end are the teardown rebuilds and gut renovations. A new home with radiant heat, an EV charger or two, and serious landscape lighting doesn't run on a standard service — that's where our panel and service upgrades up to 400 amps come in, along with clean remodel wiring coordinated with builders and designers.

Either way, the fundamentals matter more in Glencoe than most places: long wire runs across big lots, moisture near the ravines, and overhead ComEd lines threading through mature trees that come down in storms. Every service call includes a free once-over of your panel and visible wiring, so you know where your house actually stands.

Common Electrical Problems We See in Glencoe

  • Original wiring hiding behind beautiful walls. Plenty of Glencoe homes from the 1910s and 1920s still have runs of cloth-insulated wire spliced into newer circuits from decades of remodels — and the weak points are usually hidden in walls and ceilings.
  • Panels that haven't kept up with the house. An old 100-amp service, a fuse box, or a Federal Pacific panel can't safely carry today's kitchens, EV chargers, and cooling loads — we replace these regularly across the North Shore.
  • Storm outages on wooded lots. Much of Glencoe is fed by overhead lines running through mature oaks and elms, so heavy wind and ice storms knock out power here more often than in flatter, newer suburbs. It's why standby generators are one of our most-requested jobs in town.
  • Failing outdoor and landscape circuits. Long runs across large lots, ravine-side moisture, and decades-old buried cable add up to dead fixtures and GFCIs that trip every time it rains.
