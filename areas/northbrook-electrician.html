Common Electrical Problems We See in Northbrook

  • Original panels in 1950s-70s homes — including Federal Pacific breaker boxes and old fuse panels — that are undersized for modern loads and, in Federal Pacific's case, known for breakers that fail to trip when they should.
  • Storm outages on tree-heavy streets. Northbrook's mature canopy and overhead lines mean a strong storm can take the power out for hours or days, which is why standby generator calls spike here every summer and winter.
  • Not enough service capacity for how people live now. A 60- or 100-amp service that handled a 1965 ranch fine starts tripping once you add central air, a finished basement, a kitchen remodel, or an EV charger.
  • Aging branch wiring behind the walls — worn insulation, ungrounded two-prong outlets, and circuits that have been extended and tapped over decades of remodels.
  • Voltage surges riding in on the lines after storms and grid switching, which quietly shorten the life of furnaces, sump pumps, and everything with a circuit board in it.
