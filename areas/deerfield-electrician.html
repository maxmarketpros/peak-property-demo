Electrical Work in Deerfield Homes

Most of Deerfield went up in the postwar building boom — colonials, split-levels, and ranches from the 1950s through the '70s. Those houses were wired for a different era: a 60- or 100-amp panel, a handful of circuits, and nowhere near the load a modern family puts on a home. A lot of the original panels are still hanging on, and some are Federal Pacific units, a brand with a well-documented history of breakers that fail to trip. We replace these panels all over the North Shore, and it's one of the most worthwhile upgrades an older Deerfield home can get.

The other thing about Deerfield is water. Anyone who has been through a heavy spring storm here knows why so many basements run sump pumps, battery backups, and dehumidifiers. That equipment is only as reliable as the circuit feeding it — a sump pump sharing a breaker with half the basement is a problem waiting for the worst possible night. Dedicated circuits and generator hookups are steady work for us in this part of Lake County.

Add in Metra commuters trading gas cars for EVs, and there is plenty of reason to give a 60-year-old electrical system a hard look. We include a free panel-and-wiring once-over on every service call, so you know exactly where your house stands.

Common Electrical Problems We See in Deerfield

  • Original panels at the end of their life. A 1960s split-level still running its factory panel is relying on 60-year-old breakers — and if it's a Federal Pacific, those breakers may not trip when they should. We swap these for modern 200-amp panels, with service upgrades up to 400A when a home needs it.
  • Sump pumps on shared or undersized circuits. Deerfield's flooding history means basements here depend on their pumps, and a pump sharing a circuit with a freezer or workshop outlets can go dark from one tripped breaker in the middle of a storm.
  • No good place to plug in an EV. Postwar garages were wired for a light bulb and a door opener, not a Level 2 charger. Adding one usually means a new 240V circuit — and sometimes panel work first.
  • Aging or missing smoke and CO detectors. Plenty of family homes here still have detectors past their 10-year lifespan, or none hardwired at all. Illinois law requires carbon monoxide detectors near sleeping areas, and interconnected hardwired units are the standard worth having.
  • Flickering lights and warm outlets. In houses this age, it's often tired connections, overloaded circuits, or aluminum-era wiring issues — small symptoms that deserve a look before they become bigger ones.
