Electrical Work in Highland Park Homes

Highland Park is one of the prettiest towns on the North Shore, and a lot of that comes down to the tree canopy — mature oaks and maples arching over just about every street. The trade-off is that when a summer storm rolls off the lake, those same trees take down lines and knock out power. It's no accident that generator installs and storm-related emergency calls make up a big share of our work up here.

The housing stock is a mix, and each era brings its own electrical quirks. Around Ravinia and the older neighborhoods, you'll find homes dating to the early 1900s — beautiful houses that have often been renovated two or three times, sometimes with wiring from several different decades layered together. Elsewhere in town there's plenty of mid-century construction: ranches and split-levels from the '50s and '60s, many still running on 100-amp services with their original panels. We replace a lot of Federal Pacific panels and old fuse boxes in Highland Park, and both are worth dealing with before they become a problem.

Whatever era your house is from, every service call includes a free once-over of your panel and visible wiring, so you know exactly where you stand. Estimates are always free — call (773) 987-7677.

Common Electrical Problems We See in Highland Park

  • Extended storm outages. Highland Park's heavy tree cover is hard on overhead power lines, and outages here can drag on longer than in denser suburbs — which is why so many homeowners here ask us about standby generators.
  • Federal Pacific panels and fuse boxes. A lot of the town's older and mid-century homes still have original panels, and Federal Pacific breakers in particular have a documented history of failing to trip. We replace these all the time.
  • Undersized 100-amp services. Mid-century ranches and split-levels were wired for a different era — add central air, an EV charger, and a modern kitchen, and a 100-amp service runs out of headroom fast.
  • Layered, aging wiring in early-1900s homes. Houses near Ravinia have often been through multiple renovations, leaving a patchwork of old and new wiring that shows up as flickering lights, dead outlets, or warm switch plates.
