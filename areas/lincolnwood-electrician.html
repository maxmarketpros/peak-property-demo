Electrical Work in Lincolnwood Homes

Lincolnwood was largely built out in one big postwar push, and it shows in the electrical systems we open up here. Those solid 1950s brick ranches — and the split-levels and Georgians mixed in with them — were wired for a different era. A 100-amp service felt generous when a house had one TV and a fridge. Today that same panel is trying to feed central air, a finished basement, a kitchen full of appliances, and maybe an EV charger in the garage, and it simply wasn't sized for the job.

We also still find fuse boxes and Federal Pacific panels behind basement walls in Lincolnwood. Federal Pacific breakers have a documented history of failing to trip under fault conditions, and most insurance companies flag them. If your panel says FPE or Stab-Lok on it, it's worth a look — we do a free panel-and-wiring once-over on every service call, so you'll get a straight answer without paying for an inspection.

And then there's the Lincolnwood Towers holiday tradition. If your block goes big on lights every December, you already know what happens when thousands of bulbs share two exterior outlets: tripped breakers, extension cords everywhere, and a GFCI that gives up in the first cold snap. Dedicated exterior circuits and weather-rated outlets are a small project that makes that whole season easier.

Common Electrical Problems We See in Lincolnwood

  • Original 100-amp panels running out of room. Most of Lincolnwood's housing went up in the 1950s, and a lot of it still has the original service — fine then, undersized now for central air, modern kitchens, and EV chargers.
  • Federal Pacific panels and old fuse boxes. These turn up regularly in homes of this era, and FPE breakers are known for failing to trip when they should. Insurers often require replacement before renewing a policy.
  • Overloaded exterior circuits during the holidays. Elaborate light displays are a point of pride in neighborhoods like Lincolnwood Towers, but most of these homes were built with one or two outside outlets never meant to carry that load.
  • Two-prong outlets and aging receptacles. Ungrounded outlets are common in original rooms that were never remodeled, which is a problem for surge protectors and modern electronics.
  • Flickering lights and warm switch plates. In houses this age it's often worn devices or overloaded circuits — usually a simple fix, but worth having looked at promptly.
