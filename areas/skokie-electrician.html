Electrical Work in Skokie Homes

Skokie is where our shop sits and where Johnny and the crew live and work, so we know these houses inside and out — often literally. Most of the village went up fast in the 1940s, 50s, and 60s, which is why block after block is lined with brick bungalows, georgians, and ranches that all share a similar electrical story: solid construction, but wiring and panels designed for a household that owned one TV and maybe a window fan.

A lot of those homes are still running on their original 60- or 100-amp service. Plenty still have fuse boxes in the basement, and we regularly find Federal Pacific panels — a brand with a well-documented history of breakers failing to trip. One of our Skokie customers had exactly that: an aging Federal Pacific panel we replaced with a new Siemens panel. That kind of job is bread and butter for us here.

Every service call includes a free once-over of your panel and visible wiring. If you're not sure what's behind that gray door in your basement, have us look while we're already there — it costs you nothing, and in a housing stock this age it's worth knowing.

Common Electrical Problems We See in Skokie

  • Fuse boxes and Federal Pacific panels. Much of Skokie's housing was wired before circuit breakers were standard, and Federal Pacific panels installed in later decades have a known record of failing to trip — we replace both regularly.
  • Undersized 60-100A service. Original postwar service can't comfortably run central air, a finished basement, a modern kitchen, and an EV charger at the same time, so breakers trip and lights dim when big loads kick on.
  • Two-prong outlets and ungrounded circuits. Common in original bungalow and georgian wiring, and a real problem once you start plugging in computers, sump pumps, and kitchen appliances.
  • Storm-related damage. Mature trees are one of the best things about Skokie's older blocks — until a storm brings a limb down on a service line. We've handled emergency calls here after storm and tree damage, and we answer the phone 24/7.
  • Aluminum and deteriorated wiring in mid-century additions. Connections loosen over decades, which shows up as warm outlets, flickering, or a faint burning smell — all worth a call before they become worse.
