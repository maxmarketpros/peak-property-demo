Electrical Work in Wilmette Homes

Wilmette is two towns in one, electrically speaking. East of Ridge Road, much of the housing stock went up in the early 1900s — big frame and brick homes built when a house needed power for lights and not much else. A lot of them still carry pieces of that era: knob-and-tube runs in the attic, cloth-insulated wire behind plaster walls, and services that were sized for a fraction of what a modern household draws. West of Ridge, the postwar homes are younger, but plenty of them still have 100-amp panels working far harder than they were meant to.

Then there's the water. Wilmette homeowners know what a heavy summer storm can do to a basement, and the village has spent years on stormwater projects for good reason. When your sump pump is the only thing between you and a flooded basement, the wiring behind it matters — a pump sharing a circuit with a freezer or a workbench is a pump that can trip offline at the worst possible moment.

We work in these houses all the time. Every service call includes a free panel-and-wiring once-over, so before we leave you'll know exactly what shape your electrical system is in — no pressure, just a straight answer.

Common Electrical Problems We See in Wilmette

  • Knob-and-tube and cloth-insulated wiring in pre-war homes east of Ridge Road. The insulation gets brittle with age, and many insurers now flag or refuse it — we assess it honestly and can rewire in phases.
  • Undersized services and old fuse boxes. A 60- or 100-amp service that was fine in 1925 can't comfortably run central air, a modern kitchen, and an EV charger — we upgrade services up to 400 amps.
  • Federal Pacific and other obsolete panels from mid-century updates. These breakers have a documented history of failing to trip under fault, and we replace a lot of them in this area.
  • Sump pumps on shared or ungrounded circuits. Given Wilmette's flooding history, a pump that trips its breaker during a storm is a real risk — a dedicated circuit fixes it.
  • Detached alley garages with little or no power. Common in east Wilmette, and usually the first hurdle when a homeowner wants an EV charger or a real workshop.
