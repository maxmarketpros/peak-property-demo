Electrical Work in Glenview Homes

Glenview is really two electrical markets in one town. The Glen and the subdivisions built around it in the late '90s and 2000s have modern 200-amp services and wiring that's generally in good shape — what those homeowners call us for is adding things: a Level 2 EV charger in the garage, recessed lighting in a family room that came builder-basic, or a standby generator. The panels can usually handle it, but load calculations still matter once you stack a charger on top of central air and an electric range.

East Glenview is a different story. The ranches and split-levels that went up in the '50s and '60s often still run on 100-amp services, and we regularly find fuse boxes and Federal Pacific panels behind those basement doors. Federal Pacific breakers have a documented history of failing to trip, which is why we replace so many of them. Homes from the later '60s can also have aluminum branch wiring that deserves a professional look.

And because Glenview sees steady teardown-and-rebuild activity, we do a lot of new-construction and major-remodel wiring here too — for homeowners building custom and for buyers who want a new house wired right the first time. Every service call includes a free panel-and-wiring once-over, so you know where your house actually stands.

Common Electrical Problems We See in Glenview

  • Federal Pacific panels and old fuse boxes in east Glenview's post-war ranches. These went in by the thousands in the '50s and '60s, and Federal Pacific breakers are known for failing to trip when they should — we replace a lot of them.
  • 100-amp services that can't keep up. A ranch that was fine with 1960s loads starts tripping breakers once central air, a modern kitchen, and a basement remodel get added on top.
  • No garage circuit for an EV charger. Even in The Glen's newer homes, builders rarely ran a 240-volt line to the garage, so a dedicated circuit is almost always part of a charger install.
  • Storm outages under Glenview's mature trees. ComEd's overhead lines and big old parkway trees make standby generators a common request, especially for homes with sump pumps.
  • Builder-grade lighting in otherwise updated homes. Lots of 2000s-era houses here have great bones but dim, dated fixtures — recessed LED retrofits are one of our most-requested jobs in town.
