Electrical Work in Winnetka Homes

Winnetka's housing stock is beautiful and, electrically speaking, demanding. Most of the village went up between the early 1900s and the 1930s — sprawling Tudors, Georgians, and Colonial Revivals that were originally wired for a few lights and an icebox. A century later, those same homes are running central air, home gyms, heated garages, EV chargers, and finished third floors. The original service and much of the branch wiring were never built for that, and every renovation layered on since has had to work around what came before.

One thing that surprises even some North Shore electricians: Winnetka isn't ComEd territory. The village runs its own municipal utility, Winnetka Water & Electric, so any service upgrade or meter work gets coordinated with the village rather than with ComEd. We handle that coordination — the permits, the scheduling, the disconnect and reconnect — so a panel or service upgrade doesn't turn into a homeowner's part-time job.

Whether it's a full 200A-to-400A service upgrade to support an addition, a careful rewire that respects original plaster, or low-voltage lighting across a deep lot, this is the work we do week in and week out. And every service call includes a free once-over of your panel and wiring, so you know where the house actually stands.

Common Electrical Problems We See in Winnetka

  • Original knob-and-tube or cloth-insulated wiring. Plenty of pre-1930 Winnetka homes still have it live behind the plaster. It wasn't dangerous when new, but brittle insulation and buried junction points are — and many insurers now flag it at renewal.
  • Undersized electrical service. A 100-amp service was generous decades ago; it's not enough for a Winnetka home with an EV charger, a home gym, and an addition. We upgrade services up to 400 amps and coordinate the work with Winnetka Water & Electric.
  • Federal Pacific panels and old fuse boxes. Homes updated mid-century often got panels that are now known fire risks. We replace a lot of these, and we'll tell you honestly whether yours is one of them.
  • Renovations stacked on renovations. Big old homes get remodeled every generation, and each round leaves behind spliced circuits, orphaned wiring, and overloaded runs. Untangling that is normal work for us.
  • Failing landscape lighting on large lots. Deep setbacks and mature trees mean long buried cable runs — and root damage, corroded connections, and tired transformers that leave half the yard dark.
