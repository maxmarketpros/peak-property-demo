Electrical Work in Northfield Homes

Northfield is a small village, and most of its housing tells the same story: solid mid-century ranches and split-levels from the 1950s and 60s, plus a share of newer rebuilds and additions. Those original homes were wired for a different era — a 60 or 100-amp service was plenty when the biggest loads in the house were a range and a window air conditioner. Today that same ranch is running central air, a finished basement, a home office or two, and maybe an EV charger in the garage. The panel and service feeding the house are often the weak link.

We see plenty of original equipment in homes this age, including Federal Pacific panels and old fuse boxes that insurance companies increasingly flag. If your house still has one, it's worth a look — and every service call we run includes a free once-over of your panel and visible wiring, so you'll know where you stand either way.

The other big driver in Northfield is remodeling. Kitchens and basements from the 60s get opened up, and suddenly you need recessed lighting, dedicated appliance circuits, and more outlets than the original builder ever imagined. That's bread-and-butter work for us — and no job in Northfield is too small. A single dead outlet or one flickering fixture is a perfectly good reason to call (773) 987-7677.

Common Electrical Problems We See in Northfield

  • Undersized 60 or 100-amp services in original mid-century ranches. They were standard when these homes were built, but they run out of headroom fast once you add central air, a sump pump, and a modern kitchen.
  • Federal Pacific panels and old fuse boxes still in service. They're common in homes of this era and a known safety and insurance headache — we replace a lot of them and will tell you honestly whether yours needs to go.
  • Dim, dated kitchen and basement lighting. Homes from the 50s and 60s often have a single ceiling fixture per room, which is why recessed LED lighting is one of our most-requested jobs during Northfield remodels.
  • Not enough outlets where you actually need them. Original ranches were built with one or two receptacles per wall, so extension cords and power strips end up doing work they were never meant to do.
  • Ungrounded two-prong outlets and missing GFCI protection in kitchens, baths, and garages — typical for homes that haven't had their wiring touched since they were built.
